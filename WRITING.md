# JULZ Writing & Editorial Guide (WRITING.md)

This document outlines the exact rules, tone structures, sentence rhythms, and vocabulary coordinates required to write essays and articles in the custom JULZ editorial style. It is designed to allow any human writer or AI model to replicate this writing style to a tee.

---

## 1. Core Editorial Philosophy

Writing for JULZ is not merely providing technical information; it is presenting a **philosophical declaration**. The writing must feel urgent, authoritative, evocative, and grounded in structural reality. It stands in opposition to corporate marketing fluff and generic SEO-farmed content.

### The Tone Matrix
* **Intellectual & Urgent:** High-level conceptual thinking matched with immediate, civilizational needs.
* **Rebellious yet Grounded:** Critiquing monopolistic tech structures while offering feasible, concrete engineering solutions.
* **Biophilic & Industrial:** Blending natural organic patterns (soil, mycelium, wood) with industrial computing vocabulary (servers, iron, code compiles, routing tables).
* **Covenant-First:** Framing relationships, products, and software as long-term commitments (covenants) rather than transactional exchanges.

## 2. Structural Blueprint of an Article

Every JULZ article must follow a strict, four-stage narrative rhythm:

```
[ STAGE 1: The Historical Hook ]   # Start with a philosophical or historical comparison (e.g., enclosure of common lands)
              │
[ STAGE 2: The Modern Enclosure ]  # Expose how modern centralized tech replicates this trap (financial/cognitive dependency)
              │
[ STAGE 3: The Technical Blueprint]# Introduce concrete, local-first, self-hosted solutions (ZimaOS, homelabs, KVM)
              │
[ STAGE 4: The Sovereign Call ]    # Tie back to JULZ (Kenya, 2016/2023) and invite the reader into the "Crucible"
```

## 3. Sentence Rhythm & Variation (Sentence-Varied Spec)

To sound human, authoritative, and engaging, you must **obsessively vary sentence lengths**. Never write three sentences of similar length in a row. 

### Rhythmic & Perspective Patterns
* **The Punchy Declaration:** Follow a complex sentence with a very short, absolute statement.
  * *Example:* "We were promised infinite scalability, zero maintenance, and cost efficiency. **But promises, when left unchecked, have a habit of turning into enclosures.**"
* **Banning Em-Dashes (No AI Slop):** Avoid em-dashes (`—`) entirely. They are a primary signature of AI generation. Use formal punctuation (colons, semicolons, parentheses) or split ideas into short, punchy independent sentences instead.
* **Perspective Integration:** Naturally mix first-person ("we", "I"), second-person ("you"), and third-person ("builders", "JULZ") views. Avoid over-indexing on first-person singular ("I") to prevent sounding self-centered. Balance Stephen's leadership with the collective guild voice.
* **The Staggered Stack:** Cycle between:
  1. A long, descriptive setup (25–35 words).
  2. A medium, explanatory connection (15–20 words).
  3. A short, hammer-blow conclusion (4–8 words).

## 5. Style Breakdown: "The Great Cloud Exit" Case Study

We analyze the article *The Great Cloud Exit* to decode how these rules are applied in practice:

### 1. The Hook (Historical Parallel)
Instead of starting with "In this post, we discuss why companies are leaving the cloud," start with a broad, historical concept of ownership vs lease:
> *"For the last fifteen years, the technology industry operated under a singular, unquestioned dogma... But promises, when left unchecked, have a habit of turning into enclosures."*

### 2. The Anchored Reference
Always anchor arguments in real-world engineering actions and quote technical authoritarians (e.g. DHH at 37Signals, Daniel Miessler on AI):
> *"No one has articulated this trap more clearly than **David Heinemeier Hansson (DHH)**... By purchasing their own Dell servers and co-locating them in standard data center racks, DHH estimated they would save **$1.5 million annually**."*

### 3. The Shift from Virtual to Tactile
Create contrast between floating cloud terms ("virtual instances") and hard, physical hardware ("iron", "NUCs", "desks"):
> *"Today, the revolution is silent, small, and highly efficient, driven by the rise of **Mini PCs**... sitting silently on a desk... a developer can own a Mini PC that runs circles around virtualized cloud instances."*

### 4. The Local Connection & Covenant Call
Always end by anchoring the article back to the physical founding coordinates of JULZ (Kenya, 2016/2023) and presenting a call to action framed as entering the "crucible":
> *"At **JULZ**, founded in Kenya in 2016 and established in 2023, this movement is the very core of **The Forge**... The cloud was a convenient detour, but the exit is clear... it is time to bring your data home."*

## 6. Prompting Guide for AI Copy Subagents

If you are delegating article writing to an LLM, prepend your instructions with this prompt block:

```markdown

Your writing style is highly sentence-varied, using structural hooks, historical enclosures parallels, and technical anchoring.
Avoid marketing fluff (no exclamation marks, no "delightful", no "revolutionizing").
Use tactile, biophilic, and industrial vocabulary (iron, code compile, mycelium, covenant, enclosure).
BANNED: Do not use em-dashes (—) anywhere in the copy. Use colons, semicolons, parentheses, or direct sentence breaks instead.
PERSPECTIVE: Mix first-person ("we", "I"), second-person ("you"), and third-person ("builders", "JULZ") perspectives naturally. Do not overuse first-person singular ("I") to prevent a self-centered tone.
Vary sentence lengths strictly: follow a 30-word complex explanation with a 5-word absolute punchline.
Refer to DHH, Basecamp, CasaOS, or real-world homelab parameters when writing about self-hosting.
```
